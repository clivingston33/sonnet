Sonnet and the alternatives

Sonnet is one of several open-source, self-hosted servers for books. They overlap, and the right one depends on what is in your library. This page says plainly where Sonnet fits and where another project is the better choice.

In one table

ProjectCentres onPick it when
Sonnet Ebooks, audiobooks, PDFs and manga in one library, with reading goals You want one app for reading and listening, for a household, with almost no setup
Kavita Manga, comics and ebooks Your library is mixed comics and books and you want a mature reader for both
Komga Comics and manga Your library is mostly comics and manga
Audiobookshelf Audiobooks and podcasts You mostly listen, and want native phone apps
Calibre-Web A web interface for a Calibre library You already manage your ebooks in Calibre, or need to send books to an e-reader
Jellyfin Films, TV and music Books are a small corner of a video library

What Sonnet does differently

  • One library for reading and listening. EPUB, PDF, CBZ and TXT open in the built-in reader, and M4B, MP3, M4A, FLAC, OGG, Opus and WAV play in the same app.
  • Goals and stats. Daily and yearly goals, streaks, and charts of reading time, which count on their own while a book is open.
  • Profiles and families. Up to 8 profiles on one sign-in, and families that share shelves, recommendations and reviews.
  • Nothing to set up. The desktop app can keep a private library on your computer with no server at all, or host it for your network with one click.
  • No API keys. Covers, ratings, genres and series are looked up without registering anywhere.

Where the others are ahead

Sonnet is new. Be aware of what it does not have yet:

  • No native phone apps. On phones Sonnet installs from the browser to the home screen.
  • No OPDS feed and no e-reader sync, so it does not send books to a Kindle or Kobo, or feed apps such as KOReader.
  • Fewer formats. No MOBI, AZW3 or CBR; convert those first.
  • Username and password only. No LDAP or single sign-on.
  • A young project with one developer, where the others have years of use behind them.
