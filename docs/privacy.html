Privacy

Sonnet is software you run yourself. There is no Sonnet account and no Sonnet service: the people who make it never see your library, your reading or your password.

What is stored, and where

Everything is kept on the computer that runs your server: accounts, shelves, ratings, reviews, progress, reading time and the book files. Passwords are stored hashed, and so are sign-in sessions. Whoever runs the server can read its database, as with any self-hosted software.

What the server sends

Sonnet has no analytics and no telemetry. A server talks to the internet only to look books up:

ServiceForWhat it is sent
Open LibraryCovers, descriptions, ratings, new and trending booksA book’s ISBN, or its title and author; what you type in Search
WikidataSeries and a book’s place in oneA book’s title and author
Audible’s public catalogueDetails of audiobooksAn audiobook’s title and author
AniListManga and manhwaA title, or what you type in Search
Google BooksDetails the others lackA title and author, and only if the server was given a GOOGLE_BOOKS_KEY

These requests come from the server, not from your device, and say nothing about who is reading. Each profile can turn lookups off under Settings → Online.

The desktop apps

The apps look for Sonnet servers with a broadcast that stays on your own network. They do not check for updates and send nothing else.

Building from the source

Sonnet is built with Next.js, whose build tool reports anonymous usage to its maker unless NEXT_TELEMETRY_DISABLED=1 is set. That is the tool, while it builds; a running server sends nothing.

This website

It is hosted by GitHub Pages and loads its fonts from Google Fonts, so both see your address when you visit. It sets no cookies and runs no scripts.
